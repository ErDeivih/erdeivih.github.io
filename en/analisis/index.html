How much of the Fondo Popular catalogue is complete

Of the 867 competitions in the catalogue, how many have all their groups complete and how does that change by season?

Where the data comes from
Canonical table published by the Fondo Popular project on 10/11/2026: 15 seasons, 867 competitions and 206 complete ones.
What I did
I loaded the per-season table into NumPy, computed the coverage of each season and the weighted average by number of competitions, not the average of percentages.
What I take away
Overall coverage is 23.8 %. It is not uniform: 2020-21 is the most complete (16 of 31, 51.6 %) and 2019-20 the least (1 of 56, 1.8 %), because that season never closed. Recent seasons are lower because they are still being collected.

Python · NumPy · matplotlib

Goals, xG and home advantage

Does what teams create (xG) look like what they end up scoring, and how much does playing at home matter?

Where the data comes from
CC0 dataset from Data School: 380 matches with goals, shots, xG and result.
What I did
With NumPy I computed the average number of goals, the split between home win, draw and away win, and the correlation between match xG and actual goals.
What I take away
Matches average 1.89 goals; the home side wins 45.8 %, 30.0 % are draws and the away side wins 24.2 %. xG explains part of what happens (r = 0.44), but not enough to predict a single match: it is a signal, not a promise.

Python · NumPy · pandas · matplotlib

Sales of three neighbourhood stores

Which days does a neighbourhood shop sell more, and how much does each store weigh in the total?

Where the data comes from
CC0 dataset from Data School: 180 days of sales for three stores between January and June 2026.
What I did
With NumPy I summed revenue per store and computed the average per weekday by grouping the dates.
What I take away
The three stores sell similarly (between €62k and €65k in six months), but the day matters: Saturday and Sunday are the best (€1,331 and €1,180 on average) and Tuesday the worst (€809). The weekend concentrates the takings.

Python · NumPy · pandas · matplotlib

Return and volatility of a portfolio

If I had invested €1 in each asset 60 months ago, how much would I have and with what volatility?

Where the data comes from
CC0 dataset from Data School: 120 months of monthly returns for six assets.
What I did
With NumPy I computed the cumulative product of (1 + return) and annualised volatility as the monthly standard deviation times the square root of 12.
What I take away
The index ended flat (−1.3 % a year, 6.7 % volatility) and the best asset returned 9.3 % a year with 21.3 % volatility. More return came with more swings, and two stocks lost close to 40 % over the period.

Python · NumPy · pandas · matplotlib
