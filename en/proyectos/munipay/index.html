MuniPay grew out of my football team’s accounts: fines, payments and expenses kept in a spreadsheet. The application brings those records together and lets each player check their position.

What it is, what it is for and what I built

What it is. A web and Android app to keep a sports team’s fines, payments and shared expenses.

What it is for. To check the team’s accounts without depending on a spreadsheet and to export them to Excel when needed.

What I built. The product, the Flutter front end, authentication and data on Firebase, the Excel export and the Android release.

What I did not do. There are no real charges or payment gateway: it records movements. I have not reviewed it for commercial use or for many teams at once.

Manage team accounts

Administrators record fines, payments and shared expenses. Players join with an invitation code and can check their outstanding amounts.

Summaries update when data changes. Accounts can also be exported to Excel for review outside the application.

Web and Android

The web version is available here and works in the browser.

Download the Android version from GitHub. Both versions share a Flutter codebase.

How it is organised

The application separates administrator and player actions. Firebase manages accounts and team data; the interface displays records and summaries.

Published web version and an Android application available to download.

Team accounts

Fines, payments and shared expenses can end up scattered across messages and spreadsheets. MuniPay brings them together so administrators can keep accounts and players can check their position.

Records

  • Team and members: who belongs to the group and their role.
  • Fines: amounts recorded by administrators for each player.
  • Payments: records for tracking amounts paid.
  • Shared expenses: team costs split between members.

Review and export

Summaries show each player’s and the team’s position. Excel export provides a way to review data outside the application.

One application for web and Android

Flutter and Dart keep both versions in one codebase. Public distribution includes a web application and an Android APK.

Accounts and data

Firebase Authentication handles access and Firestore stores teams, members and records. The interface receives data updates and displays summaries.

Access rules distinguish administrative actions from player queries.

Export

The application generates Excel files for reviewing accounts outside the interface. Implementation details are available on GitHub.
