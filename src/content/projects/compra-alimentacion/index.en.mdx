Cesta answers one concrete question: "this shopping list — where is it cheapest, and how much do I
save?". Without inventing prices, without counting conditional offers as a real discount and without
comparing euros per kilo with euros per litre.

## What it is, what it is for and what I built

**What it is.** A weekly grocery comparator across six supermarkets: search a product, compare unit price and keep lists and price history.

**What it is for.** To decide which chain makes the weekly shop cheaper and to compare like with like: same product, same unit, same day.

**What I built.** Catalogue collection and normalisation, cross-chain matching with barcode ground truth, the application backend and front end, and the published demo with its empty, loading and error states.

**What I did not do.** I do not count conditional offers as a discount or invent prices when the data is missing. There is no native app and no user accounts in the demo: the demo data is an embedded sample.

## The problem

Doing the weekly shop well is a data decision that nobody solves from a phone today: six chains,
prices that change daily, formats that are not comparable (a one-litre carton is not a 33 cl can),
offers that only apply with a loyalty card and a history the shopper cannot consult. The chains offer
no consumer API, so the only lawful route is collecting the public catalogue and working with it
carefully.

## Approach

- **Shopping list** with an estimated price, products by aisle and suggestions.
- **Comparison** with savings, breakdown and a bar per chain; price per unit on every line.
- **History**: lowest price seen with its date, and a warning when a product returns to its low.
- **Suggestions** to repeat the previous shop and **CSV export** of the list.
- **Daily pass** at 06:00 that publishes the day's result; the app runs in Docker and starts with a
  double click on `Cesta.cmd`.

## Data and data model

- **Store:** a working SQLite database, Parquet and DuckDB for analysis and PostgreSQL for the API.
- **Collection:** HTTP with `httpx` and `curl-cffi`, plus a real browser for Alcampo's WAF; a daily
  pass orchestrated locally.
- **Product identity:** by **EAN** when available (48.5% of the catalogue) and by matching rules when
  not. Precision is measured against barcode truth: **91.1%** over 4,804 pairs.
- **Comparison unit:** euros per kilo or per litre on every line, so a carton is not compared with a
  can.
- **Explicit rules:** card, coupon or online-only offers are not subtracted; a missing value is
  declared as a gap, never filled with zeros.
- **Measured volume:** 6 chains, 65 days and 2,866,370 history rows; 39,164 comparable products with
  a price and 8,846 priced in two or more chains.
- **KPI per chain and day:** coverage, freshness, comparability and quality, each with its number
  and verdict.

## What I learned building it

- **KPIs and metrics:** daily coverage and freshness, comparability per chain and a quality figure
  per date.
- **Data quality and debugging:** identity by EAN, unit rules, coverage guards and a measured — not
  estimated — matching precision.
- **ETL:** six sources collected, normalised into a shared contract and published into a daily
  history.
- **Data models and Data Marts:** price history, comparable catalogue and comparison aggregates.
- **Reports and decisions:** a home page with the basket cost, a per-chain comparison and a product
  sheet with history. On the example basket of 8 products, the possible saving is €6.78 (−28%)
  against the most expensive chain.

## Real state

**Published demo:** [cesta-demo](https://erdeivih.github.io/cesta-demo/) (verified 200 on 14/11/2026). It covers the four states —normal, empty, loading and error— served by the demo itself via `?demo=vacio`, `?demo=carga` and `?demo=error`; measured at 390 px with no horizontal scroll and no covered controls (0/14, 0/5, 0/0 and 0/1).

**Done and verifiable:** functional end to end, checked on 2026-11-10 against the production app and
the day's real catalogue (enter, list, compare, suggestions and CSV export), with the automated flow
passing 11 of 11 steps and the daily pass published. 1,502 Python tests, 269 web component tests and
404 JavaScript logic tests are green.

**Pending:** the public demo and the repository are already published and contain no personal data.
The full app is still local-first: with the PC switched off there is no full app and the phone
connects through Tailscale. Three product decisions, a scheduled database backup and a weekly budget
with price-rise alerts are still open.
