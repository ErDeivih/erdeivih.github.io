MuniPay grew out of my football team's accounts: fines, payments and expenses kept in a spreadsheet. The application brings those records together and lets each player check their position.

## What it is, what it is for and what I built

**What it is.** A web and Android app to keep a sports team's fines, payments and shared expenses.

**What it is for.** To check the team's accounts without depending on a spreadsheet and to export them to Excel when needed.

**What I built.** The product, the Flutter front end, authentication and data on Firebase, the Excel export and the Android release.

**Decision I can point to:** I kept the Excel export because the team already worked from a spreadsheet: the app replaces the mess, not the habit of checking the accounts.

**What I did not do.** There are no real charges or payment gateway: it records movements. I have not reviewed it for commercial use or for many teams at once.

## Manage team accounts

Administrators record fines, payments and shared expenses. Players join with an invitation code and can check their outstanding amounts.

Summaries update when data changes. Accounts can also be exported to Excel for review outside the application.

## Web and Android

[The web version is available here](https://multas-c2fed.web.app) and works in the browser.

[Download the Android version from GitHub](https://github.com/ErDeivih/MuniPay/releases/latest). Both versions share a Flutter codebase.

## How it is organised

The application separates administrator and player actions. Firebase manages accounts and team data; the interface displays records and summaries.

## Development

The application is published. I continue to review management workflows and the presentation of accounts.
